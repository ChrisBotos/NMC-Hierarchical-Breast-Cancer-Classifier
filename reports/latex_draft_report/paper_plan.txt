RESEARCH QUESTION
-----------------
Wessels et al. (2005) demonstrated that simple classifiers with univariate
feature filtering outperform complex pipelines on continuous expression data.
When classifying breast cancer molecular subtypes (HER2+, HR+, Triple Negative)
from discrete, high-dimensional aCGH copy-number profiles with limited sample
size, does this principle hold, and how do feature redundancy and multi-class
structure affect classifier selection?

GOALS
-----
- Answer the research question with evidence from both flat and hierarchical
  experiments
- Show that the answer DEPENDS on problem structure: RF > NMC flat, NMC > RF
  hierarchical
- Demonstrate that the hierarchical decomposition is not just an engineering
  trick but reveals the true structure of the classification problem
- Show that correlation-based merging is a principled preprocessing step
  inspired by CGHregions
- Identify chr17q12 (ERBB2/HER2 amplicon) as the best single biomarker
- Report honest accuracy estimates with appropriate statistical tests
- Produce 2-4 gorgeous, publication-quality figures that tell the story

STORYLINE
---------
The paper reads as a discovery narrative: we set out to test Wessels' principle,
found an unexpected result, investigated why, and discovered that the answer
depends on how you decompose the multi-class problem.

Act 1: Setup (Introduction)
  - Wessels says simple wins. Does it hold for discrete aCGH data?

Act 2: First experiment (flat 2x2) seems to CONTRADICT Wessels
  - RF (complex) beats NMC (simple). Wessels was wrong?

Act 3: Investigation reveals the 3-class structure is the confounder
  - HER2+ is trivially separable (100% recall everywhere)
  - The real problem is HR+ vs TN, buried under HER2+ dominance
  - KW ranking dominated by chr17 ERBB2 signal, masking chr12q/chr5q HR+/TN signal

Act 4: Hierarchical decomposition RESTORES Wessels' prediction
  - Stage 1 (HER2+ vs rest): trivial, fixed KW+RF k=5
  - Stage 2 (HR+ vs TN): NMC > RF (reversal!)
  - With the trivial class removed, the simple classifier wins - exactly as
    Wessels predicted

Act 5: Resolution
  - Wessels was right, but only after proper problem decomposition
  - Multi-class structure and feature redundancy interact to mask the true
    classifier dynamics

METHODS
-------
Subsection: Data description
  - 100 labelled training samples, 57 unlabelled validation
  - 244K aCGH platform, segmented into 2834 genomic regions
  - CN calls: -1, 0, 1, 2
  - class distribution: 32 HER2+, 36 HR+, 32 TN (balanced)
  - hg18/NCBI36 coordinates

Subsection: Label-free region merging
  - motivation: adjacent spatial autocorrelation r=0.92 within 0.1 Mb
  - cite CGHregions (van de Wiel & van Wieringen 2007)
  - algorithm: greedy one-pass per chromosome, merge adjacent if r > 0.8
  - threshold justification: 91% of adjacent pairs have r > 0.8 (same CN event)
  - result: 2834 regions -> 273 consensus segments (90% reduction)
  - label-free: uses only CN correlation, no class labels -> no leakage
  - representation: median CN of constituent regions per sample
  - signal preservation: top KW features unchanged, PCA structure preserved

Subsection: 2x2 experimental design
  - table: (KW, EN) x (NMC, RF)
  - KW: Kruskal-Wallis H-statistic ranking, top-k selection, cite KW (1952)
    - why appropriate: non-parametric, handles ordinal CN, 3-class natively
    - hyperparameter: k (tuned via inner CV)
  - EN: Elastic Net logistic regression, cite Zou & Hastie (2005)
    - L1+L2 penalty, grouping effect for correlated features
    - hyperparameters: C, l1_ratio (tuned via inner CV)
  - NMC: Nearest Mean Classifier (centroid-based)
    - zero hyperparameters, linear boundary
    - directly referenced by Wessels et al.
  - RF: Random Forest, cite Breiman (2001)
    - 500 trees, fixed hyperparameters (not tuned - avoid inner CV overfit)
    - non-linear, captures interactions

Subsection: Hierarchical classifier
  - motivation: HER2+ recall = 100% in all flat pipelines -> trivially separable
  - 2-class KW ranking surfaces different features (chr12q, chr5q vs chr17)
  - Stage 1: HER2+ vs rest, KW+RF k=5, fixed (no tuning needed)
  - Stage 2: HR+ vs TN, one of the 4 pipeline variants
  - Stage 1 always perfect (BA=1.0 in all folds)

Subsection: Cross-validation scheme
  - nested stratified repeated 5-fold CV
  - outer loop: unbiased BA estimation (200 repeats x 5 folds = 1000 evals)
  - inner loop: hyperparameter tuning (5-fold on outer train)
  - feature selection INSIDE each outer fold (cite Ambroise & McLachlan 2002)
  - scoring metric: balanced accuracy
  - FIGURE: workflow diagram showing nested CV + hierarchical architecture

Subsection: Statistical comparison
  - Friedman test for overall pipeline differences
  - Nadeau-Bengio corrected t-test for formal pairwise claims
  - paired bootstrap CIs as pragmatic distinguishability measure
  - cite Nadeau & Bengio (2003), Bouckaert & Frank (2004)

RESULTS
-------
Subsection: Flat 2x2 experiment (50 repeats)
  - TABLE: 4 pipelines, mean BA, std, median, mean features selected
  - KW+RF best (0.791), KW+NMC worst (0.631)
  - RF >> NMC regardless of feature selector (+16 pp for KW)
  - feature selector matters less: KW vs EN only 2 pp difference
  - Nadeau-Bengio: only KW+NMC vs KW+RF significant
  - APPARENT conclusion: Wessels was wrong - RF wins

Subsection: HER2+ dominance and the case for hierarchical decomposition
  - HER2+ recall = 100% across all 4 pipelines
  - real bottleneck: HR+ vs TN confusion (TN recall 0.62-0.64)
  - 3-class KW ranking dominated by chr17 ERBB2 (11/30 top features)
  - 2-class (HR+ vs TN only) KW ranking: zero chr17 features, dominated by
    chr12q (23/30) and chr5q (7/30)
  - biological signal for the hard problem is buried by the trivial class

Subsection: Hierarchical classifier results (200 repeats)
  - TABLE: 5 base pipelines + plateau variants, BA2, combined BA
  - NMC > RF reversal: pooled Wilcoxon p=1e-16
  - EN+NMC best base (0.729), EN+NMC(plateau) best overall (0.759)
  - Nadeau-Bengio: all pairwise n.s. (conservative correction)
  - bootstrap CIs: 38/45 pairs distinguishable
  - convergence plot: rankings stable from ~75 repeats

Subsection: Plateau ensembling and the underfitting rescue
  - EN+NMC base: 0.729 -> EN+NMC plateau: 0.759 (+3 pp)
  - explanation: inner CV on n~11 cannot reliably rank 800 EN+NMC combos
  - plateau ensemble averages over near-optimal configs, stabilising
  - gain scales with grid size (800 combos -> +3 pp, 32 combos -> +1.4 pp)
  - leakage analysis: leave-one-repeat-out plateau overlap 14.9/15 -> minimal

Subsection: Single best biomarker region
  - chr17q12 (ERBB2/TCAP locus), KW H=73.9, p=9e-17
  - standalone accuracy: perfectly separates HER2+ from rest
  - cannot distinguish HR+ vs TN alone (different genomic signal)
  - biological context: ERBB2 amplification is the defining event of HER2+
    subtype, cite Slamon (1987)

Subsection: Improvements after draft submission
  - draft -> final changes: describe what was changed and why
  - hierarchical architecture as main improvement
  - plateau ensembling for deployment stability
  - accuracy estimate update

DISCUSSION + CONCLUSION (max half page)
---------------------------------------
Para 1 - Answering the research question:
  - Wessels' principle DOES hold, but only after problem decomposition
  - the flat result (RF > NMC) was an artefact of the trivially separable
    HER2+ class inflating RF's advantage
  - in the decomposed HR+/TN problem, NMC significantly beats RF
  - this extends Wessels' finding: multi-class structure interacts with
    classifier selection

Para 2 - Issues affecting results:
  - limited sample size (n=100, only 68 for Stage 2)
  - discrete CN calls lose continuous signal
  - ~10 consistently misclassified samples (possible mislabels or biological
    discordance)
  - performance ceiling set by data, not classifier (BA2 ~0.76)
  - merging threshold (r=0.8) not sensitive but chosen from data

Para 3 - Best single biomarker:
  - chr17q12 ERBB2 amplicon is the strongest single biomarker
  - separates HER2+ perfectly but uninformative for HR+ vs TN
  - for HR+/TN, the best signal is on chr12q and chr5q

Para 4 - Future research:
  - integrate with expression data for multi-omic classification
  - investigate the ~10 hard samples for biological discordance
  - test hierarchical decomposition on larger aCGH cohorts
  - explore whether the NMC advantage extends to other discrete genomic data
