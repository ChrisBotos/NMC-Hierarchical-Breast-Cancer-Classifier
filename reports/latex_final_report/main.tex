\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\textbf{Motivation:}
Breast cancer molecular subtypes (HER2+, HR+, Triple Negative) require distinct therapeutic strategies, yet classification from discrete array CGH copy-number profiles ($n = 100$, 2,834 regions) poses a high-dimensional, low-sample-size challenge. \citet{wessels2005protocol} showed that simple classifiers with univariate filtering match complex pipelines on continuous expression data, but whether this holds for discrete aCGH data with multi-class structure has not been tested.

\textbf{Results:}
A $2 \times 2$ factorial experiment crossing two feature selectors (Kruskal-Wallis, Elastic Net) with two classifiers (Nearest Mean, Random Forest) revealed that Random Forest outperformed Nearest Mean in flat 3-class classification (BA = 0.791 vs 0.631). However, HER2+ recall was consistently high across all flat pipelines (0.764-0.998), suggesting that HR+ vs Triple Negative confusion was the true performance bottleneck. A hierarchical decomposition (Stage 1: HER2+ vs rest; Stage 2: HR+ vs TN) reversed the ranking, with Nearest Mean outperforming Random Forest in Stage 2. The best pipeline, hierarchical EN+NMC with plateau ensembling, achieved a combined 3-class balanced accuracy of 0.840 and an expected validation accuracy of 48 of 57 samples.

\textbf{Availability:}
Code and predictions are provided in the submission archive.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Breast cancer comprises molecular subtypes, including HER2-positive (HER2+), hormone receptor-positive (HR+), and triple-negative (TN), that differ in prognosis and require distinct therapeutic strategies \citep{perou2000molecular, sorlie2001gene}. Array Comparative Genomic Hybridization (aCGH) enables genome-wide detection of DNA copy-number (CN) alterations; after segmentation and calling \citep{vandewiel2007cghcall, vandewiel2011preprocessing}, it produces high-dimensional discrete profiles suitable for classification. The present study classifies breast cancer subtypes from such CN profiles using 100 labelled training samples and 57 unlabelled validation samples, a typical high-dimensional, low-sample-size challenge.

\citet{wessels2005protocol} demonstrated that simple classifiers with univariate feature filtering match or outperform complex pipelines on continuous expression data \citep{vantveer2002gene}, and \citet{haury2011influence} confirmed that univariate selection achieves competitive accuracy while offering better feature stability. However, these findings pertain to continuous data; whether they extend to discrete aCGH profiles and multi-class settings has not been tested. We therefore ask: does the Wessels principle hold for discrete CN data, and how do feature redundancy and multi-class structure affect classifier selection? To answer this, we employ a $2 \times 2$ factorial design comparing simple and complex feature selection and classification methods via nested cross-validation (CV), and explore a hierarchical decomposition motivated by the observation that HER2+ samples are trivially separable while HR+ vs TN confusion is the true bottleneck.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data description}
\label{sec:data}

The training set comprises $n = 100$ samples profiled on a 244K-probe aCGH platform, summarized into 2,834 genomic regions with discrete CN calls: loss ($-1$), normal ($0$), gain ($+1$), or amplification ($+2$). Classes are HER2+ ($n = 32$), HR+ ($n = 36$), and TN ($n = 32$). A validation set of 57 unlabelled samples is provided. Coordinates use hg18/NCBI Build 36. The analysis was implemented in Python 3.10 with \texttt{scikit-learn} 1.7.2 \citep{pedregosa2011sklearn}.

\subsection{Label-free region merging}
\label{sec:merging}

Adjacent genomic regions showed strong spatial autocorrelation (median Pearson $r = 0.92$ within 0.1~Mb). Following the label-free CGHregions approach of \citet{vanwieringen2007cghregions}, we merged adjacent regions with $r > 0.8$ by taking the median CN per sample, reducing the feature space from 2,834 to 273 segments (90.4\% reduction). The threshold was chosen because 91\% of adjacent pairs exceeded $r = 0.8$, and nested CV tests confirmed that omitting merging degraded performance by 7 percentage points (pp) ($p = 0.008$, Wilcoxon). Merging is entirely label-free and was performed before CV splits; the merging map was saved and applied to validation data at prediction time to ensure consistent segment boundaries. This is distinct from the label-dependent leakage described by \citet{ambroise2002selection}.

\subsection{Experimental design}
\label{sec:design}

Following \citet{wessels2005protocol}, we adopted a $2 \times 2$ factorial design crossing two feature selectors with two classifiers (\autoref{fig:workflow}).

\textbf{Feature selection.} The Kruskal-Wallis (KW) test \citep{kruskalwallis1952}, a univariate method in the Wessels tradition, ranks features by their $H$-statistic across the three subtypes; the top \mbox{$k \in \{5,10,15,20,30,50,75,100\}$} are retained. As a multivariate alternative, Elastic Net (EN) multinomial logistic regression \citep{zou2005elasticnet} evaluates features jointly; the top $k \in \{5,10,20,50\}$ (by summed absolute coefficients) are retained, with $C \in \{10^{-3}, \ldots, 10^{2}\}$ and $\texttt{l1\_ratio} \in \{0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9\}$ tuned in the inner CV loop.

\textbf{Classifiers.} The Nearest Mean Classifier (NMC), a simple linear method recommended by \citet{wessels2005protocol}, assigns samples to the closest class centroid with optional shrinkage $\delta \in \{\text{None}, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5\}$. Random Forest (RF), a complex ensemble, uses 500 trees with balanced class weights \citep{breiman2001random}, tuning \texttt{max\_features} $\in \{\text{sqrt}, 0.3\}$ and \texttt{min\_samples\_leaf} $\in \{1, 5\}$. Crossing the two selectors with two classifiers yields four pipelines with 32 (KW) or 800 (EN) inner CV configurations per fold.

\subsection{Hierarchical classifier}
\label{sec:hierarchical}

HER2+ recall was near-perfect across RF pipelines (0.994-0.998; \autoref{tab:results}), while HR+ vs TN confusion was the bottleneck across all flat pipelines. A binary KW ranking on HR+ and TN samples surfaced chr12q and chr5q features (\autoref{fig:features}B) instead of the chr17 ERBB2 signal dominating 3-class ranking, motivating a two-stage architecture.

Stage~1 classifies HER2+ vs rest using a fixed KW+RF model with $k = 5$ chr17q12 features at the ERBB2 amplicon ($H = 73.9$, $p = 9 \times 10^{-17}$), achieving BA $= 1.000$ in all 1,000 outer folds. Stage~2 classifies the remaining samples as HR+ or TN using one of the four pipeline variants with full inner CV tuning on non-HER2+ samples only, accessing the chr12q/chr5q signal that 3-class ranking obscures (\autoref{fig:workflow}).

\subsection{Cross-validation scheme}
\label{sec:cv}

We employed nested, stratified, repeated 5-fold CV \citep{wessels2005protocol, ambroise2002selection}. The outer loop estimates generalisation performance (50 repeats $\times$ 5 folds $= 250$ evaluations for flat; 200 $\times$ 5 $= 1{,}000$ for hierarchical). The inner 5-fold loop tunes hyperparameters, with all feature selection performed strictly within inner folds to prevent leakage. Performance is measured by balanced accuracy (BA), the arithmetic mean of per-class recalls \citep{brodersen2010balanced}. For hierarchical models, we report both the combined 3-class BA and Stage~2 BA (BA2), the latter computed only on samples routed to Stage~2 (HR+ vs TN). The workflow is shown in \autoref{fig:workflow}.

\subsection{Plateau ensembling}
\label{sec:plateau-method}

Inner CV folds in Stage~2 contain only $\sim$11 samples, insufficient to reliably rank 800 EN configurations: 303 distinct top-1 selections appeared across 1,000 folds, with negative inner/outer-test correlation ($r = -0.378$). Plateau ensembling addresses this by retaining all configurations within one pooled SD of the best mean inner-CV score (up to 15 members) and averaging their predicted class probabilities. The one-SD threshold was fixed a priori. Leave-one-repeat-out analysis confirmed plateau robustness: on average, fewer than 1 of 15 members changed when any single repeat was omitted, indicating that plateau membership is stable under resampling rather than overfit to specific fold compositions.

\subsection{Statistical comparison and final model}
\label{sec:stats}

Pipeline differences were assessed using the Nadeau-Bengio corrected $t$-test \citep{nadeaubengio2003}, which accounts for non-independence in repeated CV, and bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs; 10,000 resamples) \citep{bouckaert2004evaluating}. The final submitted model (EN+NMC with plateau ensembling) was retrained on all 100 samples with fixed plateau membership and the same region-merging map applied to validation data.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Flat 2x2 experiment}
\label{sec:results-flat}

In the flat setting, RF outperformed NMC regardless of feature selector: KW+RF achieved BA $= 0.791$ vs $0.631$ for KW+NMC (\autoref{tab:results}). HER2+ recall was high across all pipelines (0.764-0.998), but TN recall ranged from only 0.507 to 0.641 (\autoref{fig:results}B, left), identifying HR+ vs TN confusion as the bottleneck.

\subsection{Hierarchical classifier}
\label{sec:results-hierarchical}

The hierarchical decomposition reversed the classifier ranking: NMC outperformed RF in Stage~2 (HR+ vs TN), with bootstrap CIs excluding zero for all NMC-vs-RF pairs. EN+NMC(P) achieved the best BA $= 0.840$ and BA2 $= 0.759$ (\autoref{tab:results}; \autoref{fig:results}). The Nadeau-Bengio test found no significant pairwise differences, consistent with its conservative nature for repeated CV.

  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig2_results.pdf}

\subsection{Plateau ensembling}
\label{sec:plateau}

Plateau ensembling reversed the classifier ranking: base EN+NMC (BA2 $= 0.729$) trailed both KW+NMC ($0.736$) and standalone EN ($0.738$) because EN's 800-configuration grid overfits on $\sim$11 inner-fold samples (negative inner/outer correlation; Section~\ref{sec:plateau-method}), whereas KW's 32 configurations remain stable. By averaging near-optimal configurations rather than selecting the single overfit best, plateau ensembling neutralized this: EN+NMC(P) ($0.759$) surpassed KW+NMC(P) ($0.750$) and standalone EN(P) ($0.741$; \autoref{tab:results}). Yet overfitting alone does not explain the result: standalone EN(P) accesses the same 800 configurations but gains only +0.3~pp, while EN+NMC(P) gains +3.0~pp. The critical difference is the classifier. Each plateau member selects a different feature subset, producing a different NMC with centroids in a distinct subspace. Averaging their predictions is analogous to random subspace ensembling: the consensus across multiple complementary views of the data is more robust than any single projection, effectively transforming NMC from a single centroid classifier into a multi-view ensemble. EN's logistic regression, by contrast, uses regularization to implicitly weight all features, so different hyperparameter configurations converge to similar decision boundaries and averaging adds little diversity.

\subsection{Single best biomarker region}
\label{sec:biomarker}

The chr17q12 ERBB2 amplicon (KW $H = 73.9$, $p = 9 \times 10^{-17}$) enabled perfect Stage~1 separation using five features, consistent with its established role as a clinical biomarker \citep{slamon1987her2}. For Stage~2, discriminative signal resided on chr12q and chr5q (\autoref{fig:features}).

\subsection{Accuracy estimate}
\label{sec:estimate}

The expected number of correct predictions was estimated as $\sum_c \hat{r}_c \cdot (n_c/N) \cdot 57$, where $\hat{r}_c$ is the mean per-class recall across 200 repeats and $n_c/N$ the training class proportion. With HER2+ $= 1.000$, HR+ $= 0.777$, TN $= 0.741$, this yielded $47.7 \approx 48$. Sensitivity analysis under alternative class proportions produced estimates of 46-48; 48 was submitted.

\subsection{Iterative model refinement}
\label{sec:improvements}

Three methodological refinements improved accuracy beyond the initial draft submission. (1)~The flat KW+RF (BA $= 0.791$) was replaced by a hierarchical decomposition, improving the best BA to $0.824$ (+3.3~pp) and reversing the classifier ranking: NMC outperformed RF in Stage~2, consistent with the Wessels principle on a well-structured subproblem. (2)~Plateau ensembling raised EN+NMC from BA $= 0.819$ to $0.840$ (+2.1~pp) and BA2 from $0.729$ to $0.759$ (+3.0~pp). (3)~The estimate was updated from BA~$\times$~57 to a per-class weighted formula, increasing from 45 to 48.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The Wessels principle is conditional on problem structure. In the flat 3-class setting, RF outperformed NMC (BA $= 0.791$ vs $0.631$; \autoref{fig:results}A), apparently contradicting findings on continuous expression data. After hierarchical decomposition removed the trivially separable HER2+ class, NMC outperformed RF in the binary HR+ vs TN problem. The HER2+ class acted as a confounder: its perfect separability inflated RF's advantage in the 3-class setting. In both settings, feature selector choice mattered less than classifier choice, consistent with \citet{wessels2005protocol}. The discovered biomarker regions harbor genes with established roles in breast cancer biology: chr17q12 contains *ERBB2* (HER2 receptor tyrosine kinase), while Stage~2 signals map to *CDK4* and *MDM2* on chr12q (cell cycle regulation and p53 antagonism) and *PIK3R1* on chr5q (PI3K/AKT pathway activation), consistent with known molecular differences between luminal (HR+) and basal-like (TN) subtypes \citep{chin2006genomic, sorlie2001gene}.

Limitations include the small sample size ($n = 100$, 68 for Stage~2) and discrete CN representation. Approximately 10 samples were consistently misclassified ($>$80\% across 1,000 test appearances; \autoref{fig:results}B), suggesting the BA2 $\approx 0.759$ ceiling reflects irreducible noise rather than classifier capacity. Future work could integrate expression data for multi-omic classification and test whether the hierarchical NMC advantage generalises to larger cohorts. In conclusion, simple classifiers can outperform complex ones on discrete aCGH data, but only when multi-class structure is decomposed to match the underlying biology.
